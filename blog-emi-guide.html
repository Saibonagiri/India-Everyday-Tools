How to Calculate EMI: A Complete Guide for Indian Borrowers

Understand exactly how your monthly loan payment is worked out, and what actually changes it.

If you've ever taken a home loan, car loan, or personal loan in India, you've dealt with EMI — Equated Monthly Instalment. It's the fixed amount you pay every month until the loan is cleared. But most people never actually see how that number is worked out, which makes it hard to judge whether a loan offer is reasonable or to plan a budget around it. This guide walks through it in plain language.

What EMI actually covers

Every EMI payment is split into two parts: a portion that repays your original loan amount (the principal), and a portion that pays interest to the lender. In the early months of a loan, a larger share of your EMI goes toward interest, and a smaller share toward principal. As the loan progresses, that balance gradually shifts — more of each EMI starts going toward principal, and less toward interest. This is called the reducing-balance method, and it's what almost every Indian bank and NBFC uses for home, car, and personal loans.

The formula itself

The standard EMI formula is:

EMI = P × R × (1 + R)N ÷ [(1 + R)N − 1]

Where P is your loan amount (principal), R is your monthly interest rate (your annual rate divided by 12, then by 100), and N is your total number of monthly instalments (loan tenure in months). It looks intimidating written out, but the underlying idea is straightforward: it spreads your total repayment — principal plus all the interest that will accrue — into equal monthly chunks.

Why the same loan amount can have very different EMIs

Three things move your EMI up or down: the loan amount, the interest rate, and the tenure. A longer tenure lowers your monthly EMI but increases the total interest you'll pay over the life of the loan, since you're paying interest for more months. A shorter tenure raises your EMI but reduces total interest paid. This trade-off is worth thinking through carefully — a lower EMI can look more affordable month-to-month, while actually costing you significantly more overall.

What EMI calculators don't show you

A standard EMI calculation, including the one on this site, gives you the core monthly figure based on principal, rate, and tenure. It generally doesn't include processing fees, loan insurance premiums, prepayment penalties, or GST that some lenders charge separately. Always ask your bank or NBFC for the full cost breakdown, not just the quoted EMI, before signing anything.

A practical way to use this

Before approaching a lender, it helps to work backward: decide what monthly EMI actually fits your budget comfortably, then use that to figure out what loan amount and tenure combination gets you there, rather than starting from "how much can I borrow." This keeps the loan a tool that serves your plans, rather than a fixed monthly obligation that dictates them.
