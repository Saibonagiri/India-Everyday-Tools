How CGPA to Percentage Conversion Actually Works

Why the 9.5 multiplier exists, where it came from, and when it doesn't apply to you.

If you've studied under CBSE or many Indian engineering and university programmes, you've likely received a CGPA — a Cumulative Grade Point Average, typically on a 10-point scale — rather than a straightforward percentage. But plenty of job applications, further education forms, and eligibility checks still ask for a percentage figure specifically, which is where conversion comes in.

Where the 9.5 multiplier comes from

The most widely referenced conversion is: percentage equals CGPA multiplied by 9.5. This particular multiplier is associated with CBSE's own guidance for converting CGPA to an equivalent percentage for its 10-point grading scale. It's become something of a default assumption across many institutions, even outside CBSE, simply because it's the most commonly cited formula.

Why it's an approximation, not a universal rule

Here's the part that catches people out: 9.5 is not a mathematically fixed or universally mandated conversion — it's a commonly used approximation. Different universities, especially at the postgraduate or engineering level, often define their own official conversion formula, which can differ meaningfully from the 9.5 default. Some use a different multiplier entirely; others use a formula that first subtracts a constant before multiplying (something like (CGPA − 0.75) × 10, used by a few university systems).

Why this matters for official use

For casual reference — figuring out roughly where you stand, or getting a quick estimate — the 9.5 multiplier is a reasonable and widely accepted shortcut. But for anything you're submitting officially, such as a job application form or an admissions portal that explicitly asks for your percentage, it's worth checking your own university's official conversion certificate or published formula rather than relying on the general estimate. Many universities issue an official percentage-equivalent certificate alongside your transcript specifically for this reason.
