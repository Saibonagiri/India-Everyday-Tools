Understanding GST Rates in India: A Simple Guide

What GST actually is, how the common rate slabs work, and how to calculate it correctly.

GST — Goods and Services Tax — replaced a patchwork of older indirect taxes (VAT, service tax, excise duty, and others) with a single, unified tax structure across India. If you run a small business, freelance, or just want to double-check a bill, understanding the basics of how GST is calculated is genuinely useful.

The common GST rate slabs

GST in India is generally applied at one of four common rates: 5%, 12%, 18%, or 28%, depending on the category of goods or services involved. Everyday essentials are often taxed at lower rates or exempted entirely, while luxury and certain non-essential goods sit at the higher 28% slab. Rates and categorisation can change through government notifications, so it's worth checking the current applicable rate for anything specific rather than assuming it hasn't changed.

Adding GST vs removing GST

There are two directions you'll typically need to calculate. If you have a base price and need to add GST on top (for example, pricing a product before tax), the formula is straightforward: GST amount equals the price multiplied by the rate, divided by 100, and the final price is the original price plus that GST amount.

The reverse situation comes up when you have a final, GST-inclusive price — like a restaurant bill or an invoice total — and want to know how much of that was the base price versus the tax. In that case, the base price equals the final amount divided by (1 plus the rate divided by 100), and the GST portion is the difference between the final amount and that base price.

Why this trips people up

A common mistake is applying the "adding GST" formula when you actually have an inclusive price, which overstates the tax. Another common confusion is with discounts — GST is usually calculated on the price after any discount is applied, not before, so the order of operations matters when you're working out a final bill by hand.

For small business owners and freelancers

If you're invoicing clients, it's worth being explicit on every invoice about whether your quoted price is GST-inclusive or exclusive, since this avoids disputes later. Keeping a simple record of the GST rate applied to each transaction also makes filing returns considerably easier down the line.
